# Frequently Asked Questions about our Community Workshops    
    
## Welcome to the ZK AV Club Workshops FAQ page.

This section was created to answer common questions about our community workshops, including participation, hosting, scheduling, technical requirements, recordings, collaboration, and organizational processes.

The goal of this FAQ is to make workshops easier to understand, more accessible to the community, and simpler to coordinate for both participants and organizers.

Whether you are a community member, speaker, moderator, or collaborator, this page will help you better understand how the ZK AV Club workshop system works.


**- What is the Zk Av Club workshop program?**
  
  - It is a program of interactive, hands-on sessions focused on collective “learning by doing.” These are not passive presentations, but rather shared conversations and explorations—both technical and non-technical—aimed at generating useful outcomes, skills, or lasting public documentation.



**- How do I invite someone to lead a workshop?**

  - You can encourage them to submit an initial proposal (even if it’s just a rough draft) via the application link on the website (“Apply to facilitate”), or fill out the form to suggest a topic of interest (“Request a topic”).



**- How many workshops can a single facilitator lead?**

  - There is no strict limit specified on the website; accepted formats range from a single session to entire workshop series, depending on the complexity of the topic and the agreed-upon proposal.


**- What types of content and formats are accepted?**

  - The content is very broad: technical development (tools, nodes), media production, community skills (governance), or creative experiments. Formats include demonstrations, guided exercises, in-depth analyses (teardowns), clinics, or open discussions.


**- How do workshops add value to the club?**

  - They build community infrastructure and lasting public knowledge. Instead of generating ephemeral content, workshops help members solve real problems, master practical tools, and create useful documentation for the entire network.


**- What are the benefits of leading a workshop with us?**
  - Serving as a workshop facilitator at the ZK AV Club allows you to:
    1.  Reach an audience interested in privacy, ZK technology, and digital culture
    2. Create recorded, reusable content (both educational and promotional)
    3. Rely on the support of the team of coordinators to design, publish, and conduct the workshops
    4. Have the opportunity to test new ideas, topics, or tools with an active community
    5. Connect with other facilitators and members of the ecosystem communities who attend the workshops
    6. Have that content distributed through the ZK AV Club’s channels

**- How much time should a facilitator spend preparing for a workshop?**
  - This depends to some extent on each facilitator’s experience with instructional design (whether they are a beginner, intermediate, or advanced facilitator). Generally speaking, a workshop can be structured with the following preparation times:
 
    * Pre-coordination: 1–2 brief calls (20–35 minutes each)
    * General instructional design: 2–5 hours (depending on the format)
    * Technical test (if applicable): 30 minutes
    * Live workshop: 60–90 minutes (per session)
    * Approximate total: 5–8 hours total

**- Workshops require participants to prepare in advance (read materials, install software, etc.). How do you communicate this to participants and ensure they come prepared?**

  - Before the workshop, the facilitator should clearly communicate the workshop requirements. Registration forms should indicate whether participants will need specific items, such as:

    * Pre-installed software or applications
    * Any other participation requirements

**- What are the specific roles of the facilitator, moderator, and coordinators in workshops?**
  
  - Moderator
    * Supports the live experience
    * Manages interaction, questions, etc.
 
  - Facilitator
    * Expert who leads the content
    * Designs and delivers the workshop
    * Creates the call to action for the workshop
    * Designs the graphics to invite people to the workshop
    * Promotes and invites participants via social media
 
   - Coordinators
      * Handle internal aspects such as: 
      * Scheduling the workshop production process
      * Communication with the Facilitator
      * They monitor the logistics carried out by the Facilitator 
      * Review of metrics (people connected to the workshop)
      
**- I’d like to invite someone with extensive experience and an impressive resume to lead a workshop, but they’re too busy to attend lengthy meetings to plan it. How do we handle situations like this to design a workshop that’s valuable for both the Zk Av Club and worth that person’s time?**
    
   - For people like this, we suggest:
      * Send a direct invitation (don’t just send a link to the website)
      * Write a personalized message
      * Offer a concrete (not open-ended) proposal
 
   - Propose:
      * A specific topic. E.g.: “Community strategy for privacy-first ecosystems”
      * Format (e.g., hands-on workshop, guided conversation)
      * Provide a list of suggested dates for the workshop
 
   - Explain:
      * What’s in it for him? (see questions 1–2)
      * Estimated time (short)
      * How we support him from the club
 
   - Offer:
      * Joint workshop design (help with designing the flyer, registration form, promotion, etc.)
      * Flexibility in the format
      * Make a brief call to finalize details and address any questions
 
   - Recommend:
      * Don’t ask them to start from scratch.
      * Come with a nearly finalized proposal.
    
**- Can a workshop be done irl and recorded?**

  - At this initial stage of the project, we will only accept live online workshops.
